ARC IS TWO CHAINS

Circle launched Arc on 16 September 2026 and every outlet ran the same story: the institutional chain had been taken over by memecoins on day one. Twenty-seven launchpads opened within hours and Argus alone minted 83,751 tokens in the first 24 hours.

The arithmetic does not support the takeover. In its first week Arc moved $6.8B of USDC against 624M in circulation — eleven turns of its own float, which is what a settlement rail looks like. Everything traded on every DEX pool on the chain accounts for a low single-digit share of that.

The memecoin layer was not a takeover. It was a rounding error on a payments network. This page counts it, pool by pool, from the chain.

METHOD

how every number here was produced

Arc routes every Uniswap V4 swap through a single PoolManager at 0x8366a39c…e40951, with the pool id as an indexed topic. That makes the chain's entire DEX history one filtered log query. Pools are counted from Initialize events on the same contract, over the same block window, so "created" and "traded" are measured on identical ground.

No API wrapper, no paid RPC, no indexing service. The block number in the header is this page calling rpc.mainnet.arc.io directly from your browser — open devtools and watch it.

Stated limits. The scan covers blocks 21,065,860 to 22,357,859; pools created before Arc's public launch are excluded. Thirteen of 1,292 chunks failed on rate limits, so roughly 1% of the window is unscanned and the created count is a floor. Swap-derived counts come from a 4.6M-swap index and are also floors. Volume is the USDC leg of each swap, summed absolutely, so it counts both sides of a round trip.

What is deliberately not claimed. A pool with few traders is not necessarily a scam, and a high fee is not necessarily a trap — but both are facts about the pool that nobody surfaces before you trade. Fee figures are the pool's own setting; where a V4 hook sets the fee per swap the pool is marked DYN rather than given a number.
